Not recommended? How to come back stronger

What it means, how many chances you have, and what to change before your next ISSB.

Being not recommended is not a verdict on your worth. It means that on those days, the board did not see enough of the qualities it was looking for. Many officers were not recommended the first time.

How many chances?

The number of ISSB attempts is limited. For most commissions candidates get a small number of chances (commonly two for a type of commission), and some entries count separately. Your call letter and the current advert decide — read them, do not rely on rumours.

What to change

1. Write down honestly what happened each day: tests, tasks, interview, conference.

2. Find the gap between who you are and who you showed. Were you quiet in group tasks? Did your stories all end the same way? Did you exaggerate in the interview?

3. Fix the person, not the answers. Read, play team sports, take responsibility at home, help someone every week. The board measures habits, not memorised lines.

4. Keep your biodata (PIQ) consistent and true. The board compares it with your earlier attempt.

5. Practise under time — word association, situations, stories — until your first thought is a positive, practical one.

What not to do

  • Do not pay someone to “evaluate” you or predict your result. Nobody outside the board can.
  • Do not copy “recommended answers” from others — assessors have read them hundreds of times.
  • Do not change your personality for three days. Change your habits for three months.
