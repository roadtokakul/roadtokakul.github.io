Mechanical aptitude — the ideas behind it

Some initial tests, especially for the Navy, the PAF and technical branches, include mechanical comprehension. The questions use simple pictures of gears, levers, pulleys and liquids. Nine ideas cover almost all of them.

The nine ideas

  • Gears: meshed gears turn opposite ways; the smaller gear turns faster.
  • Belts: an open belt keeps the direction; a crossed belt reverses it.
  • Levers: weight × distance on one side = weight × distance on the other.
  • Pulleys: effort ≈ load ÷ number of rope sections holding the load.
  • Ramps and screws: less force over a longer distance.
  • Pressure: force ÷ area. Sharp edges and narrow heels give high pressure.
  • Liquids: pressure grows with depth, not with the shape of the vessel.
  • Heat: most things expand when heated; metals expand more than glass.
  • Floating: an object floats when it displaces its own weight of water.

Method

  • Picture the movement step by step. Trace each gear or belt with your finger.
  • With levers and pulleys, count first, then calculate.
