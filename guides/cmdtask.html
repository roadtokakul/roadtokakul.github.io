The command task, explained

You are the commander. Two helpers, one obstacle, a few minutes.

In the command task you are given an obstacle — usually a gap or a ditch to cross with planks, ropes, drums and a load — and you choose two or three other candidates as your helpers. The GTO watches how you plan, explain and lead.

How to do it well

1. Take ten seconds to study the obstacle, the material and the rules (coloured areas you cannot touch, lengths of planks).

2. Make a plan out loud in two or three sentences, then give each helper a clear job.

3. Do the hardest physical part yourself where you can.

4. If the plan fails, stay calm, say what you will change, and try again.

5. Thank your helpers at the end.

What the GTO sees

  • Whether you think before you act, and whether you can change your plan.
  • How clearly you explain, and whether you treat helpers with respect.
  • Whether you keep going when it is not working.

Choose helpers who were active in the group tasks — not your friends.
